Out-of-sample robustness test: The earlier strategies used a hand-picked list of 10 known-successful funds — that's look-ahead bias. Critical lesson

The earlier "amazing" results from buy-3+ (+13% 1Y alpha) and the staggered blend (+25% CAGR, Sharpe 0.97) were OVERFIT to a hand-picked list of 10 funds. With proper dynamic out-of-sample selection:

  • The buying signal vanishes and reverses — buy-3+ now LAGS SPY by 11 pp/yr CAGR
  • The staggered blend goes from +25% CAGR to +9.9% (UNDERPERFORMS SPY 15.5%)
  • Only the consensus 5+ signal SURVIVES — but with much smaller alpha (~1.6 pp/yr)

The genuine, robust finding from this entire project: weighted-by-conviction consensus 5+ produces ~3% CAGR alpha at 3-7Y horizons (t=+2.9 to +7.8), but the headline buying-signal results were largely an artifact of fund-selection bias.

Why this happens

Macro hedge funds' core strategies are futures, FX, and rates — none of which appears on a 13F. The 13F equity book is typically a small, often defensive sleeve of their actual book. Examples:

  • Bridgewater All Weather: diversified equity exposure as part of a balanced portfolio, not concentrated stock-picking
  • Tudor: 3,400+ holdings — a quasi-quantitative equity overlay, signal heavily diluted
  • Caxton Corp: ~10 holdings — concentrated but tiny relative to macro book
  • Brevan Howard, Element, Rokos: primarily futures-driven; equity book may be hedging, not alpha

Bottom line: the "smart money" 13F signal lives in equity-focused hedge funds (Tiger Global, Coatue, Lone Pine, Pershing Square, Viking, etc.), not in macro funds. Macro funds' 13F holdings are an actively bad input for an equity strategy — they systematically lag the market.

Methodology

Universe

10 hedge funds chosen for (a) industry prominence, (b) mix of macro / quant / multi-strat / equity L/S / activist, and (c) continuous 13F-HR filings covering 2015-Q4 through 2025-Q4. Includes Bridgewater (macro), Citadel & Millennium (multi-strat), D.E. Shaw / Two Sigma / Renaissance (quant), Point72 / Tiger Global / Viking (equity L/S), and Pershing Square (activist).

Data

SEC EDGAR 13F-HR filings, 410 filings covering 41 quarter-ends, 1.34M position rows. CUSIPs mapped to tickers via OpenFIGI free API. Prices from Yahoo Finance (auto-adjusted close as total-return proxy).

Top-50 ranking (per fund-quarter)

Composite score = 0.5 × weight z-score + 0.5 × increase signal z-score, where weight is position market value / total fund 13F value, and increase signal is (shares_t − shares_t-1) / max(shares_t-1, shares_t). New positions get +1; closed positions are excluded.

Consensus thresholds

For each quarter, a CUSIP is "consensus" at threshold N if it appears in the top-50 of N or more funds. Three portfolios run in parallel: 2+ (avg ~92 stocks/qtr), 3+ (~40), 5+ (~7).

Rebalancing

Equal-weight rebalance on the first trading day STRICTLY AFTER the last 13F filing date across the 10 funds for that quarter. We use actual filing timestamps from EDGAR — typically ~45 days post quarter-end, but as late as ~50 days. Hold until next rebalance. This guarantees no lookahead bias.

Filters

Common stock only (share_type = SH). Options (put/call) excluded. ETFs (SPY, IVV) excluded from the consensus portfolio to avoid circular benchmark.

Caveats

  • Survivorship bias: Stocks that delist/merge are dropped at rebalance (yfinance limitation). ~14% of consensus universe is unmapped/delisted. Effect modestly favorable to strategy returns.
  • Long-only view: 13F shows long US equity positions only — does not capture shorts, options hedges, or fund's actual P&L. Pershing Square's 13F closely matches its book; multi-strat 13Fs do not.
  • Concentration risk: Thr-5 holds only 4-13 stocks. High return came with 41% max drawdown vs SPY's 34%.
  • No frictions: No transaction costs, slippage, or borrow costs modeled.
  • Era-specific: Past decade was a mega-cap-tech bull market. Consensus heavily AAPL/MSFT/GOOG/META/AMZN/NVDA. Different regime would likely show different alpha.
